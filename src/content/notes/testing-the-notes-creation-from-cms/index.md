## Atomic Notes: The Building Blocks of Knowledge

Atomic note-taking is a method where each note captures a single, self-contained idea or concept. Rather than writing long, sprawling notes that mix multiple topics together, you create one note per idea—concise, focused, and written in your own words. Each atomic note should be understandable on its own, without needing extra context, which makes it easy to revisit, reuse, and connect later. This approach is a cornerstone of systems like Zettelkasten, where the real power comes not from individual notes but from the links between them.

## another paragraph

The main benefit of atomic notes is that they turn your notes into a flexible, growing network of ideas rather than a static archive. Because each note is small and specific, you can link it to related notes, combine it with others to build larger arguments, or drop it into new contexts like writing, projects, or teaching. Over time, this creates a compounding effect: the more atomic notes you make and connect, the more effortlessly new insights and connections emerge. The key habits are keeping notes short, titling them clearly, and linking generously.
